The Usual, Done Right

An AI shopping and reorder assistant for 10-minute delivery in India and grocery delivery in the US. It gets your usual basket right in one tap, and never sneaks anything into the cart.

Download the full PDF9-page PDF · October 2026 · 18 sources
How I would build it. Not something I shipped.

01

The problem

Arjun and Meera live in Gachibowli. They order three or four times a week, mostly top-ups, and every time they rebuild the same 12-item cart from scratch.

Then at checkout they find out the paneer is out of stock. The one thing dinner depended on.

Across town in Kukatpally, Lakshmi runs a joint-family kitchen. Search doesn’t understand “kandi pappu” or “chinna packet”, so her son places her orders. In Columbus, Ohio, Jess plans five dinners on Sunday night and keeps getting substitutions that ignore her son’s nut allergy.

My bet: the best grocery assistant isn’t the chattiest one. It’s the one that gets your usual basket right in one tap.

Who I’d build for first: Arjun and Meera, and Jess. Highest order frequency, clearest repeat patterns. That’s where a reorder model earns trust fastest.

Lakshmi is the bigger unlock in India. But she needs voice to be very good before she should see it.

02

India vs US

Same job, two very different markets

The job is the same everywhere: keep my kitchen stocked and help me decide what to cook. Almost everything around that job is different. The row I keep coming back to is substitution.

When does the swap happen? India · 10-minute dark store
Build basket→Swap shown, before you pay→Pay→10 to 15 min
US · shopper picks in the aisle
Build basket→Pay→Shopper picks→Item missing, swap chat mid-pick→Same day

Same feature name. Completely different product.

05

Decisions I’d defend in a review

Kept baskets, not bigger baskets

I chose “kept assisted baskets per week” as the north star. It rewards the assistant only when it saves effort and the customer agrees with what it did. Basket size is an outcome I watch, not the target.

North star: AI-driven AOVThat’s a very polite upsell engine. In India, a legally risky one.

Pre-built basket over a chat box

People start with restocking.6 A chat box makes the user do the explaining. A pre-built basket does the work for them.

A full chat interface as the front doorDeliberately not building it first.

Buy the commodity, build the moat

Speech-to-text and the base LLM get better every quarter, so I’d buy them. The purchase graph, live inventory and substitution feedback are ours. Nobody can buy those. Later, distil a small in-house model for cost.

Holdout before the first model

“Assistant users spend more” is a selection effect until proven otherwise. Heavy shoppers try new features first. A 5% holdout runs from day one, and decisions are made on holdout deltas only.

From scaling DIY vehicle inspection at ICICI Lombard (adoption grew 30%): self-serve AI wins on trust, not cleverness. Every guardrail here is also a growth feature.
06

How I’d know it works

Metrics tree
North star

Kept assisted baskets per week

Orders where the assistant built at least half the items, and none of its picks were removed, refunded or complained about.

Reach

  • Share of weekly shoppers who open it

Accuracy

  • Share of AI-proposed items kept

Speed

  • Seconds from ask to ready basket
  • p95 under 3 s text, 5 s voice (target)

Habit

  • Week-4 return rate

Guardrails. Any one of these can stop a rollout.

  • Regret: removals and refunds no worse than control
  • Safety: zero allergen misses
  • Cost: ≤ ₹1 India, ≤ $0.05 US
  • Fairness: sponsored share capped and labelled

Outcomes I read against the holdout, not the target: orders per user per month, items per order, month-3 retention, fill rate.

Roadmap

Each phase has a gate
0 to 3 months

MVP

“The usual” card and pre-checkout swaps. Text only. Hyderabad, and one US metro.

Gate: item keep rate ≥ 70%, no guardrail breach
3 to 6 months

Phase 2

Voice in Telugu, Hindi and English. Recipe to basket. US meal plans.

Gate: parsing bars met, cost ≤ ₹1
6 to 12 months

Phase 3

Opt-in auto-replenish with a price cap and edit window. Household profiles. More cities and languages.

Gate: holdout shows lift in frequency or retention
08

First 90 days

Days 1 to 30 · learn

What share of each household’s basket is actually a repeat (measure it, don’t assume it). Top null searches by language. Today’s substitution rejection rate. Build the golden sets and the holdout.

Days 31 to 60 · prove trust

“The usual” for 1% of eligible users in Hyderabad and one US metro. Item keep rate, time to checkout and removals, every day.

Days 61 to 90 · decide

Ramp to 10% only if keep rate is at least 70% and no guardrail is worse than control. Close with a one-page scale, fix or stop memo.

If I had to pick one number on day 90: the share of AI-proposed items customers keep. If people keep what the assistant picks, everything else follows. If they don’t, no amount of conversation will save it.
