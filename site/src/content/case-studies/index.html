Case studies · AI product management

How I’d build it.

Four AI products in sectors I care about: healthcare, insurance, payments and quick commerce. Each one covers the US and India, because the same idea turns into a different product in each market.

Every page has the screens, a user journey, the metrics I’d track and a roadmap. The full write-up is a PDF at the bottom of each page.

Before you read: these are self-initiated. I didn’t ship any of them. Where I lean on real experience, it’s from my 7 years in data and product, the last two and a half as a PM on AI in motor insurance at ICICI Lombard.

One rule kept showing up

I didn’t plan it, but three of the four products ended up with the same line in them. The AI can approve, route, warn or hold. It can’t be the one that says no.

In prior auth, only a licensed clinician denies. In claims, the AI can pay a clean claim but only a person can cut or reject one. In payments, the model can hold a payment, but a person or the customer makes the final call on grey cases.

It’s partly regulation and partly trust. Mostly it’s that a model score is very good at deciding which queue something goes to, and a poor reason to tell a customer no. I learned that one working on fraud models.

AI can say yes.
Only a person
can say no.
