Case study 03PaymentsUS + India

Pause

Stopping scam payments without slowing down honest ones. A real-time scam-risk and intervention layer for UPI in India, and Zelle, RTP and FedNow in the US.

01

The problem

Ramesh is 68, retired, in Hyderabad. A “CBI officer” on a video call tells him his Aadhaar is tied to money laundering. Don’t hang up. Don’t tell your family. Move your savings to a “verification account”.

He unlocks his own phone. He types his own PIN. He sends the money himself. Every security check passes, and the money is gone in seconds.

In April 2026 the RBI asked whether every person-to-person payment above ₹10,000 should be held for an hour1. This case study is my answer. Hold the few payments that look like a scam in progress, explain why in plain words, and let everything else go through instantly.

Why I picked this. I’ve worked on six ML fraud models in motor claims. Two lessons carry straight over. Fraud is rarely one red flag, it’s a few weak signals together. And every flag costs an honest customer something.

02

India vs US

Who owns the screen changes everything

India · UPI

802 mn
UPI payments a day in September 2026. 24.07 bn in the month.2
Who owns the screen
Third-party apps. PhonePe, Google Pay and Paytm carry 90%+ of UPI volume5
Shared signals
DoT’s Fraud Risk Indicator on phone numbers5, MuleHunter.AI in 26 banks8, DPIP live with 8 banks9
Economics
P2P is free to users, so friction has no fee to offset it
What I’d do
B2B2C through a big app or bank. Plug in FRI and DPIP first. Voice warnings in regional languages.

US · Zelle, RTP, FedNow

4.2 bn
Zelle payments in 2025, worth $1.2 tn.11
Who owns the screen
The customer’s bank. Zelle sits inside ~2,400 banks and credit unions12
Shared signals
Inside the Zelle network. I found no public national phone-number risk feed.
Economics
Trust is worth more per deposit customer
What I’d do
Sell to mid-size banks and credit unions. Start with the “move your money to a safe account” script.
The numbers don’t agree, and that matters. The Finance Ministry counted ₹981 crore of UPI fraud in FY253. The cybercrime portal counted ₹22,848 crore of digital payment fraud in 20241. Different scopes explain part of a 20× gap. The model learns whichever definition you feed it, so labels are a product decision.

05

Decisions I’d defend in a review

Trees first, fancy models later

I’d start with gradient-boosted trees. Fast enough for the payment path, strong on tabular data, and they give reason codes the warning and the analyst both need. A sequence model for multi-payment scams like Sneha’s comes once there’s labelled data.

Start with a deep sequence modelNo labels yet, and it can’t explain itself.

Judge the model the way the bank thinks

Not AUC. Value-weighted recall at a fixed good-user interrupt rate. The bank’s real question is: if we interrupt one honest payment in 200, how much scam money do we catch?

Optimise for “scams flagged”Flags are cheap. Money is what customers lose.

No A/B test that hides a warning

I won’t run an experiment that knowingly shows nothing on a high-risk payment. Experiments live in the middle band: which tier, which copy, how long a hold.

Fail open, carefully

If a signal feed goes down, Tiers 0 to 1 fail open and the hard-rule lists stay cached. Honest payments shouldn’t stop because a vendor had a bad night.

06

How I’d know it works

Metrics tree
North star

Scam value stopped, per month

Value stopped = attempted scam value × coverage × stop rate. Not “scams flagged”.

Coverage

  • Value-weighted recall on confirmed scams
  • Launch bar 50%+ (my target)

Stop rate

  • Share of warned users who cancel
  • By copy variant and scam type

Time to stop

  • Minutes from first risky payment to stop
  • Catches Sneha before payment three

Guardrails. These stop the team winning by interrupting everyone.

  • Good-user interrupt rate ≤ 0.5% (target)
  • P2P completion vs holdout
  • Added p99 latency under 100 ms
  • Complaints and appeals per 10k interrupts
  • No group above 2× average interrupt rate

Roadmap

Each phase has a gate
0 to 3 months

MVP

One partner, Android only. P2P to new payees above ₹2,000. Hard rules, FRI and model v0. Tiers 0 to 2. Hindi, Telugu, English. 4 to 6 weeks of shadow.

Gate: launch bar met in shadow, partner signs off on interrupt rate
3 to 6 months

Phase 2

Tier 3 holds with analyst callback, trusted contact, DPIP scores, more languages, voice warnings.

Gate: stop rate up, complaints flat, callback SLA met
6 to 12 months

Phase 3

Mule-account scoring on the beneficiary side, iOS, sequences across UPI and IMPS. US pilot with a credit union group on RTP or FedNow.

Gate: value-weighted recall 60%+, US shadow results
08

First 90 days

Days 1 to 30 · baseline

Confirmed scams a month at the partner, label delay, share with a beneficiary freeze, baseline P2P completion. Ship the hard rules on day one.

Days 31 to 60 · shadow

Value-weighted recall at 0.25%, 0.5% and 1% interrupt rates. Precision by scam type. p99 latency on live traffic.

Days 61 to 90 · first users

Tiers 1 to 2 for 10% of eligible users. Stop rate by copy, completion vs holdout, complaints per 10,000 interrupts. Ends in one call: scale, retune or stop.

The trade-off I’d defend in an interview. I’d rather ship a model that interrupts one honest payment in 200 and explains itself, than one that catches a little more and teaches people instant payments can’t be trusted. The goal isn’t more friction. It’s friction in the right place.
