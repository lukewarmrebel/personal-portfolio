Case study 01HealthcareUS + India

PreAuth Copilot

Getting patients a yes in minutes, not days. An AI copilot for US prior authorization and India’s cashless pre-auth.

Download the full PDF9-page PDF · October 2026
How I would build this. Not something I shipped.
Personas are composites. Anything marked est. is my own maths.
01

The problem

Maria runs prior auth for a 14-doctor orthopaedic group in Ohio. She has six payer portals open and she is re-typing chart notes into a form, for an MRI the surgeon already knows the patient needs.

Ravi does the same job at a 250-bed hospital in Hyderabad. His clock is louder. It’s 9 pm, a family is waiting in the lobby for discharge, and the TPA has sent one more query by email.

Two countries, same problem. Most of these delays are not hard medical calls. They are incomplete files.

So that’s the bet. Make every request complete the first time. And never, ever let the AI say no.

Why I picked this. At ICICI Lombard I’ve spent two and a half years putting AI into motor insurance: vision-based inspection, OCR at issuance, fraud models, and a claim admissibility engine with 20+ automated checks. Prior auth is the same shape. Messy documents, policy rules, someone waiting.

Same problem, a different clock

When a regulator puts a clock on a workflow, the product that makes the clock easy to meet gets bought. Both markets did that in 2024.

Reading this chart: the US problem is labour. Practices burn hours proving need. India’s problem is waiting. A family stuck at discharge, a bed blocked. Same product, different pitch.

    05

    Decisions I’d defend in a review

    Provider side first, not payer side

    I chose the clinic desk over selling to payers because that’s where the file gets made. Payer tools like Cohere Health and Availity AuthAI already speed up the payer. I’d rather build the packets those engines approve fastest.

    Payer-side auto-approval in v1Slows down proving the core loop.

    The AI can never deny

    This one isn’t a trade-off for me. California’s SB 1120 requires physician oversight, CMS says algorithms can assist but each decision rests on the patient, and 6 in 10 physicians already worry AI will push denials up.

    Model recommends denials “for review”A copilot that can say no loses the provider’s trust on day one.

    Rules for lookups, models for reading

    Eligibility, benefits, code validity and package-rate maths are lookups. Rules are cheaper, faster and you can audit them. Models only get the messy part: notes, scans, criteria written in prose like “failed six weeks of conservative therapy”.

    One LLM does the whole requestSlower, pricier, and impossible to audit line by line.

    Buy the model, build the policy library

    A frontier model through a HIPAA-eligible cloud in the US, India-region hosting for India. What I’d build is what compounds: payer criteria versioned by effective date, the citation layer, the gap-check model and the eval harness.

    Train our own modelWasted money. The policy library and outcome data are the moat.

    Left out of v1 on purpose: pharmacy prior auth (different rails, already crowded), payer-side auto-approval, a US patient app. Each one slows down proving the core loop.
    My rule from claims work: automate the checks before you automate the judgment.
    06

    How I’d know it works

    Metrics tree

    I picked a north star that only moves if the copilot is accurate and actually used. Volume of packets drafted would have been easier to grow and told me nothing.

    North star

    First-time-right authorizations

    Share of requests approved with no query or pend, inside the payer’s clock. Patient, coordinator and payer all feel it move.

    Complete packets

    • Packet completeness score
    • Critical-field accuracy
    • Evidence citation precision

    Fast decisions

    • Share sent via API or NHCX
    • Real-time response rate
    • Hours to final decision

    Real usage

    • % of auths started in the copilot
    • Edits per packet
    • Minutes per auth

    Guardrails. Any one of these can stop a rollout.

    • AI-caused wrong approvals in audit
    • Unsupported statements under 0.5%
    • Zero PHI incidents
    • Denial rate parity by age, sex, language, region
    • Coordinator time per auth never goes up
    07

    Roadmap

    Each phase has a gate
    Weeks 0 to 6

    Discovery

    15 interviews per market. Map 200 real requests. US: imaging and ortho surgery. India: five planned procedures, three hospitals, one TPA.

    Gate: baseline time per auth, query rate, turnaround
    Months 2 to 4

    Shadow MVP

    Packet builder and gap check run beside the team. People still submit the old way.

    Gate: every eval bar met on live traffic
    Months 4 to 9

    Assisted

    Submit from the copilot. Tracking, query and appeal drafts. India discharge fast lane with WhatsApp updates.

    Gate: first-time-right +15 pts, minutes per auth −40% (targets)
    Months 9 to 18

    Connected

    Payer FHIR Prior Auth APIs in the US (2027) and NHCX in India. Real-time clean-lane pilot with one payer.

    Gate: 50%+ of volume on APIs, zero guardrail breaches
    08

    First 90 days

    Days 1 to 30 · baseline

    Minutes per auth, query rate, first-time-right and turnaround for two US specialties and one TPA. Label 500 cases. Sign three design partners per market.

    Days 31 to 60 · shadow

    Run silently on live traffic. Field accuracy, citation precision and gap-check recall against real queries. Fix or cut anything under the bar.

    Days 61 to 90 · assisted pilot

    About ten coordinators. Targets: 60%+ of auths started in the copilot, minutes per auth down 40%, zero guardrail breaches. Then go or no-go.

    If a coordinator in Ohio gets an hour of her day back, and a family in Hyderabad leaves the hospital before dinner instead of after midnight, this product sells itself. The AI is the easy part. Trust is the product.
